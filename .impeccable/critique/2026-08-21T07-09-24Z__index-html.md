## Design Specificity Verdict

The site is clearly more authored than templated overall. Warm paper, operational teal, salmon signals, square rules, condensed display type, and the public-safe workflow establish a coherent “Operational Field” world. It avoids the common AI portfolio failures: no bento dashboard, glass cards, gradient blobs, rounded pills, generic SaaS feature grid, or ornamental glow.

The remaining template signal is subtler: the site repeatedly converts every kind of evidence into the same ruled ledger, mono label, oversized uppercase heading, and three-row narrative scaffold. That is no longer generic SaaS, but it is a recognizable AI-generated “editorial engineering portfolio” convention. The interface sometimes demonstrates a design system more loudly than it demonstrates the individual work.

The deterministic scan returned zero findings, but it ran in degraded regex mode because parser modules were unavailable. It could not evaluate selector matching, custom properties, or computed contrast, so the empty result is an undercount rather than a clean bill of health. Rendered evidence showed no horizontal overflow at 1440, 768, or 375px; ordered headings; complete images; strong contrast; correct mobile focus trapping; and accessible reduced-motion behavior. Mutable browser injection failed, so no reliable user-visible overlay exists.

## Overall Impression

The opening is genuinely strong: the hero, workflow, Selected Work transition, and Microsoft-first hierarchy feel deliberate and recruiter-aware. Word Lawrie also has a convincing media-led identity. The biggest remaining opportunity is to stop the interior pages and lower homepage from feeling like content poured into a reusable portfolio apparatus. The design peaks early, becomes résumé-like through Experience/About, and turns procedural inside Azure and CMV.

## What’s Working

1. **The first viewport has authority.** The name, thesis, workflow trace, and the beginning of Selected Work coexist without the giant-empty-hero problem. Microsoft is unmistakably primary.
2. **Selected Work is not a generic card grid.** The full-width teal, paper, and salmon fields create hierarchy through composition and material, not rounded boxes or hover lifts. Word Lawrie’s real imagery gives the portfolio welcome proof and texture.
3. **The foundation is production-grade.** Responsive layouts do not overflow, mobile targets are at least 44px, focus management is strong, contrast passes AA, reduced motion leaves content visible, and case navigation provides clear exits.

## Priority Issues

### 1. [P1] The case studies are one template wearing three different projects

**Why it matters:** Azure and CMV both follow the same sequence: oversized uppercase title, four-cell metadata ledger, colored “system” field, three identical narrative rows, confidentiality strip, and next-case band. By the second case, the visitor is reading the scaffold rather than discovering how Ethan thought. For hiring managers, this reduces distinctiveness precisely where project-specific reasoning should be strongest.

**Fix:** Keep shared navigation and basic metadata, but give each case its own narrative grammar. Azure should behave like a trace investigation: handler → span → signal → operational decision → UAT, with annotations tied to the path. CMV should lead with constraints, capture-to-storage architecture, and the explicitly planned rollout state. Word Lawrie should keep the media-led release dossier. Do not force all three through the same three-row story component.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### 2. [P2] Experience and About create a long, mechanically repeated résumé valley

**Why it matters:** Both chapters use the same enormous condensed uppercase statement, right-hand deck, long rule, and ledger below. About then repeats the education/GPA from its intro in the profile ledger, uses a postage-stamp portrait, and resolves the competition results into three equal cells. The page loses emotional momentum after Selected Work and begins to feel auto-formatted.

**Fix:** Preserve the strong Experience rows. Redesign About only: use a larger, more intentional portrait crop; one concise personal/engineering statement; education once; and a compact skills rail. Make the competition results an authored ranking moment rather than another equal three-cell ledger. Let this chapter be human and asymmetric instead of another section-intro template.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### 3. [P2] The rule/ledger/mono grammar is overused

**Why it matters:** Case metadata, evidence lists, the CMV architecture mini-grid, signal matrices, profile ledger, achievement ledger, contact labels, and confidentiality notes all use variations of the same thin rule plus tiny uppercase mono label. It is coherent, but coherence has become sameness. This is the clearest remaining AI-template artifact: every fact is styled as “structured evidence,” whether or not it benefits from that treatment.

**Fix:** Reserve the fully boxed ledger for one high-value proof moment per case. Let supporting evidence become typographic captions, margin notes, short ranked lists, or project-specific diagrams. Remove redundant labels and avoid converting every four-item set into equal cells.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### 4. [P2] Typography and motion maintain one cadence for too long

**Why it matters:** Condensed uppercase display type announces the hero, both homepage chapters, contact, every case title, Word statements, and the 404. Meanwhile important metadata often drops to 0.7rem mono. The contrast is strong locally but monotonous globally: loud headline, tiny label, ruled content, repeat. Motion has a convincing hero trace, yet most later elements fall back to generic grouped fade/rise reveals and a thin peripheral scroll rail that resembles a creative-portfolio progress garnish more than meaningful system state.

**Fix:** Keep condensed uppercase for true peaks only. Give About and some case subheads a sentence-case sans voice; increase important metadata and desktop navigation sizing; and vary measure more intentionally. Retain the hero choreography, but remove blanket reveals from ordinary rows. Use scroll-linked motion only where a project relationship changes—such as trace stages becoming current or an architecture path resolving—not as a page-edge progress line.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset` followed by `$impeccable animate`

### 5. [P3] The ending and small utility details are less authored than the opening

**Why it matters:** “Start a conversation” plus a split contact form is a familiar portfolio ending. The large teal field and tall empty message area create dead space, while Résumé and LinkedIn repeat actions already available in the header. On mobile, LinkedIn incorrectly shows a downward arrow rather than an external-link arrow. The 404 footer’s “Back to top” action is meaningless on a one-screen recovery page. Desktop navigation also misses the project’s own 44px target contract in several places, although it still passes WCAG’s 24px minimum.

**Fix:** Make the closing copy more specific to Ethan’s working style without naming target roles; compress the form vertically; and turn the close into a decisive final thought rather than a generic invitation. Correct the LinkedIn arrow specificity, restore 44px desktop targets, and replace the 404 back-to-top utility with a useful route.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

## Cognitive Load

**Low, with one meaningful failure.** Single focus, grouping, hierarchy, sequential reading, recognition, and working-memory demands are generally strong. The failure is progressive disclosure/chunking in the lower homepage and case bodies: duplicated education, dense skill strings, repeated evidence cells, and multiple equal narrative rows ask the visitor to process more résumé detail than the hierarchy warrants. The mobile menu also exposes six destinations, although Résumé is visually separated and the list remains manageable.

## Emotional Journey

- **Peak:** Hero → operational workflow → Microsoft Selected Work. This feels confident and specific.
- **Sustained proof:** The three full-width work fields, especially Word Lawrie’s real imagery.
- **Valley:** Experience and About repeat the same typographic machinery, then the case bodies repeat the same narrative machinery.
- **Ending:** The teal contact field restores brand color but closes with generic portfolio language rather than a memorable final point of view.

## Persona Red Flags

### Time-poor hiring manager

The homepage communicates cloud/tooling focus quickly, but Azure and CMV reveal limited new visual evidence after their strong heroes. Repeated system fields and three-row narratives can read as a designed wrapper around sparse public proof. Education is duplicated while the programming results are visually understated, so scan priority is not fully aligned with interview value.

### Jordan, a recruiter without deep platform context

Terms such as multi-handler, UAT, CI, OpenTelemetry, Grafana, retrieval-augmented, and API boundary are not consistently translated into outcomes. The site is navigable, but Jordan may understand the technology list more readily than the concrete organizational value. Confidentiality notes are accurate yet visually resemble defensive legal disclaimers.

### Casey, a distracted mobile visitor

The mobile hero is clear, but the 679px workflow panel pushes Selected Work to roughly 1391px down the page. Casey must consume the full diagram before reaching the portfolio index. Project entries and the contact form are long vertical commitments. The full-screen menu works well, but its incorrect LinkedIn arrow creates a small trust-breaking inconsistency.

### Sam, a keyboard or low-vision visitor

Semantics, contrast, focus trapping, reduced motion, labels, and mobile target sizes are strong. Remaining polish issues are the undersized desktop navigation targets relative to the site’s own 44px contract and very small mono labels. These are not demonstrated WCAG failures, but they lower comfort at zoom and weaken the otherwise careful system.
